02 — 1 — The electrode

Two needles in the stem.

A sprung clip closes on the stem and drives two titanium needles through the outer tissue into the conducting core. The clip is 3D-printed and has its own thin wire back to the house module. There is no arm.

03 — 2 — The probe

Moisture, and the salts in it.

In the pot, a resistive board behind a bevelled window reads moisture. Two turns of bare copper read how well the soil conducts; salts raise conductivity, so it's how we watch what the plant is being fed.

04 — 3 — The pod

A little house for the brain.

The module is a separate little house: an ESP32-S3 under a gable roof with two 70 mm solar panels. The soil probe and the stem clip connect to it by wire, and the tube runs to the probe. It samples soil, air, light and the electrode, stamps the time, and packs the result into one 46-byte frame with a CRC-16 on the end. A battery powers it, and the roof panels top it up so it can run for up to two months.

05 — 4 — The link

Forty-six bytes through the air.

The frame goes out over Bluetooth LE in one short burst, then the radio sleeps again: low energy, still quick. Stakes on one block form a mesh, so a block shares one weather. A block is only described when at least three stakes from two accounts have reported.

One stake, a whole block

A fungus needs weather, not luck.

Hours of high humidity, a temperature band, a canopy that stays wet. Those conditions are shared across a block, so one well-placed station speaks for the plants around it. Several stakes turn a number into a map.

What it does not do

The needles read the plant they are in. They don't sense a neighbour's tissue, and they can't name a fungus species. A block warning comes from shared weather, and is raised only when two kinds of evidence agree.

Privacy, enforced twice

A block is only described when at least 3 stakes from at least 2 accounts have reported, checked in the API and again in the database view.

The 46-byte frame

Small enough for Bluetooth. Strict enough to trust.

Every reading travels in one little-endian frame. A CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE covers bytes 0 to 43. Absent sensors use sentinels (0xFFFF, 0x8000, 0xFF) instead of fake zeros, and the Swift and Kotlin encoders are tested to be byte-identical.

The tier rule

Every metric says how real it is.

BUILT

You can trust the number

Soil moisture and conductivity, air temperature, humidity and pressure, light. Printed, wired and logged.

PROTOTYPE

Read it as a direction

Stem impedance and the twin-pump loop. They work on the bench; impedance is reported as trend and timing, never a calibrated figure, and never a stress the soil and air can't corroborate.

ROADMAP

Simulated, and labelled

Nitrate, phosphate, potassium and sap flow. The UI never shows these as measurements; they come from a simulator and say so. Electro-culture ships off, logged as an experiment.
