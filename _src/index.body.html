01 · The farm

One module. One plant. A whole farm around it.

A farm has thousands of plants, and a single RootStake module sits on just one of them. Scroll to follow what that one module can see, from the whole field down to a single cell.

02 · The plant

The house, the probe and the clip belong to one plant.

The house module stands beside the plant. The soil probe is in the soil, joined by wire and the watering tube. The stem electrode is on the stem, joined by its own thin wire. Everything the module knows comes from this one plant.

03 · The stem

Two micro-needles go in.

The clip closes and the two extremely thin titanium needles slip through the outer skin of the stem, about a pinprick each, and stop in the conducting tissue.

04 · Inside the stem

Skin, cortex, and a ring of pipes.

Cut across, a stem has an outer skin (epidermis), a cortex of soft cells, a ring of vascular bundles, and a pith in the middle. In each bundle the xylem carries water up and the phloem carries sugars. The needle tips sit in that ring.

05 · The cells

Down to single cells.

Each cell has a wall, a thin membrane inside it, a big water-filled vacuole, a nucleus and chloroplasts. The xylem vessels are wide, hollow pipes; the phloem sieve tubes have companion cells that look after them. The membranes are what the current has to deal with.

06 · The current

Slow goes around. Fast goes through.

A tiny current runs between the two needles. At 1 kHz it can't cross membranes, so it winds around the cells. At 100 kHz it passes straight through them. Healthy tissue shows a big difference between the two.

07 · The fungus

A fungal colony moves in.

Some fungal diseases colonise the xylem. Thread-like hyphae grow up through the vessels, branch, and push through the walls into neighbouring cells, until a colony is living inside the plant. Spores ride the sap, and hyphae cross the vessel walls at tiny pits. All of this happens inside the stem, long before it shows on a leaf.

08 · The damage

The tissue stops behaving like tissue.

Hyphae and spores clog the vessels, and the plant plugs its own pipes in defence. The tissue browns and the membranes of nearby cells start to leak. The wall between "around" and "through" gets thinner, and that is exactly what the current feels.

09 · The signal

The two readings move together.

Leaky membranes no longer block the slow current, so the gap between the 1 kHz and 100 kHz readings closes. The module spots that shift in the trend over time.

10 · The alert

One plant speaks up. A whole crop is saved.

Fungi move fast, from one plant to the next, and by the time leaves show it, much is already lost. One RootStake module on just one plant can raise the alarm early, giving growers time to act and protect tons of crop that would otherwise be destroyed.

The system

The app and the stake are one loop.

A reading leaves the stem, crosses the soil, rides 46 bytes of Bluetooth to your phone, becomes a verdict, and comes back down as water and feed through a bore in the probe. Then the app checks it worked.
