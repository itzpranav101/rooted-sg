The story

Singapore counts what it plants.

Since 2020 it has handed out 860,000 free seed packets. Nobody records how many of those plants are still alive.

Still growing 100%
01 — A field of seedlings

Everyone starts optimistic.

70.9% of the 3,043 people we surveyed want to grow at home. They get a packet, a pot and a good intention.

02 — Then, quietly

Most of them go out.

23.6% tried to grow something and stopped. They didn't know what grows here, when to water, or whether the plant was even healthy.

03 — The idea

Count what we keep alive.

Rooted tracks plants over months. RootStake is how we measure it, so a number only grows while the plant is still alive.

What we don't claim

We'd rather be checked than impressed.

Rooted won't solve food security.

It aims to raise participation, knowledge and local growing activity. Small household actions add up, but they aren't a farm.

Stem impedance is a prototype.

It works on the bench. We report trend and timing, never a calibrated figure.

Ion sensing and sap flow aren't built.

They sit on the roadmap and are labelled as simulated everywhere they appear.

One stake can't detect a neighbour's fungus.

Block-level warnings come from shared weather, not from tissue sensing.

Electro-culture isn't a claim.

It ships off, logged per sample as an A/B experiment.
