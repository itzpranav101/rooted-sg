One part in the pot.

02 — Two halves

Split face-up. No supports.

The halves part to show the channels inside, with the green leaf crown riding above. Fourteen iterations to get the point right.

03 — Chisel point

Saw teeth bite packed mix.

A 40 mm toothed chisel on a 4 mm pitch, so it goes in by hand without bending. The 4.5 mm bore exits at the centre of the nose.

40 mm point4 mm pitch4.5 mm tube
04 — Two readings

Moisture prongs. Copper turns.

A resistive board behind a bevelled window reads moisture. Two turns of bare copper read how well the soil conducts, and salts raise conductivity.

05 — Soil-line mark

Push to the mark.

Every reading after comes from the same depth, so a number today means the same as a number last week.

06 — The dose

Water reaches the roots.

Two small pumps feed one Y-connector into the probe's bore. The dose leaves at the nose, where the roots are. Every dose is logged with its volume.

Twin 5 V pumps

The module

A little house for the brain.

Together with the Rooted SG app, this module is Meco (heymeco.xyz). The module is a small house that stands on its own: a gable roof carries two 70 mm solar panels over an ESP32-S3. There is no arm and no gooseneck. The soil probe and the stem electrode are separate parts that connect back to the house by a thin wire, and by the watering tube for the probe. No screws anywhere: parts press-fit, twist-lock or hot-glue.

Prototype — Stem electrode

Ask the stem, not the leaf.

A sprung clip closes on the stem and drives two titanium needles through the outer tissue and into the conducting core.

1 kHz

Around the cells.

At 1 kHz the current can't cross a living membrane, so it threads the wet space between the cells. Watch the arc bow outward.

100 kHz

Straight through.

At 100 kHz it passes through the membranes. The ratio is the measurement. We never claim it senses a neighbour's plant or locates a fungus.
