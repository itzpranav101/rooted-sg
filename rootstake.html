04 — Two readings

Moisture prongs. Copper turns.

A resistive board behind a bevelled window reads moisture. Two turns of bare copper read how well the soil conducts, and salts raise conductivity.

05 — The tube goes in

Down the back, to the nose.

The 4.5 mm clear tube slides down its channel in the back half until it sits flush at the centre of the nose. The other end leaves the top and runs to the module's pumps.

06 — The moisture sensor

The resistive probe drops in over the tube.

The resistive moisture board slides into the bevelled window directly over the tube. Its two exposed tines sit in the soil through the window; the board's wire leaves from the top and goes back to the house module.

07 — The pH electrode

It slots in beside them.

The pH electrode has its own channel, so it seats with its glass bulb at the same depth as the tines and never touches the tube. It fits with no gap; the halves then close over all three parts.

Own channelSame depth as the tines
08 — Soil-line mark

Push to the mark.

Every reading after comes from the same depth, so a number today means the same as a number last week.

09 — The dose

Water reaches the roots.

Two small pumps feed one Y-connector into the probe's bore. The dose leaves at the nose, where the roots are. Every dose is logged with its volume.

The module

A little house for the brain.

Together with the Rooted SG app, this module is Meco (heymeco.xyz). The module is a small house that stands on its own: a gable roof carries two 70 mm solar panels over an ESP32-S3. There is no arm and no gooseneck. The soil probe and the stem electrode are separate parts that connect back to the house by a thin wire, and by the watering tube for the probe. No screws anywhere: parts press-fit, twist-lock or hot-glue.

Power and radio

A battery, stretched by the sun. A radio that mostly sleeps.

01 — Battery + solar

The module runs on a battery, but two 70 mm solar panels on the roof keep topping it up through the day, so the battery drains far more slowly than it would alone. With the panels helping, the module can run for up to two months before the battery needs any attention.

Battery2 × 70 mm solarUp to two months
02 — Low-energy radio, still quick

Everything runs on Bluetooth Low Energy. The module sleeps almost all of the time, wakes, reads its sensors, packs one 46-byte frame and sends it in a quick burst, then goes straight back to sleep. A tiny frame and a short radio-on window keep the energy use very low without making the update slow: the app still hears from the stake quickly.

Prototype — Stem electrode

Ask the stem, not the leaf.

A sprung clip closes on the stem and drives two titanium needles through the outer tissue and into the conducting core.

1 kHz

Around the cells.

At 1 kHz the current can't cross a living membrane, so it threads the wet space between the cells. Watch the arc bow outward.

100 kHz

Straight through.

At 100 kHz it passes through the membranes. The ratio is the measurement. We never claim it senses a neighbour's plant or locates a fungus.
